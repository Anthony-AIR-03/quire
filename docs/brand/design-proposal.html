Why the town library kept its card catalogue

Volunteers check the drawers every morning. The council asked twice if they could go.

By Ada Morrow · 12 October 2026 · about 7 minutes

The drawers have not moved in sixty years. Every weekday morning, before the doors open, a volunteer pulls one at random and checks that the cards still match the shelves.

The council asked twice whether the catalogue could go. Twice, the readers said no. The second time, they brought a petition with four hundred names, written on index cards.

A slower way to find a book

Nobody here argues that the drawers are faster than the search screen. They are not. The point, the head librarian says, is what you pass on the way.

You look for one card and read the six around it. A reader who came for a cookbook leaves with a history of salt.

You look for one card and read the six around it.

Head librarian, Northfield

Who keeps it going

Eleven volunteers share the work. Most are retired. Two are students who started for a school project and stayed. They type new cards on a typewriter the library bought in 1974.

The cost is small: card stock, ribbons for the typewriter, and one afternoon a week. The council's last review put it at less than the cost of the coffee machine.

Quire's note

This piece argues the catalogue helps readers find books they were not looking for. The next two pages report the trend more widely and argue the opposite.

For now the drawers stay. The volunteers have started a new set, for books donated this year.
